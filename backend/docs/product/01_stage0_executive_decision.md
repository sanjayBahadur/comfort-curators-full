# Comfort Curators: Curators Portal and Workforce Expansion

Status: Stage 0 decision package  
Launch geography: Uttar Pradesh, India  
Planning date: 2 August 2026  
Initial operating base: approximately 30 short-term-rental properties

## Executive decision

Proceed, but narrow the first launch to a property-operations company supported by software. Do not launch five businesses at once.

Comfort Curators should initially sell one outcome: reliable, documented short-term-rental operations for independent homeowners. The Curators Portal, Jarvis, field workforce, supply catalog, and micro-inventory should all serve that outcome. A public labor marketplace, teenage delivery program, imported e-bike financing business, advertising network, laptop resale program, and external accounting service should remain outside the launch scope.

The viable structure is:

1. A core field crew of fixed-term employees for cleaning, inspection, restocking, and simple property tasks.
2. Independent specialist businesses for plumbing, electrical, appliance, pest, structural, and other skilled work.
3. Company-owned, compliant 250 W e-bikes issued as work equipment, beginning with two locally supported bikes.
4. A homeowner subscription or management commission, with cleaning, consumables, repairs, and goods priced separately and transparently.
5. A small catalog and micro-fulfillment point, not a full warehouse.
6. A property-scoped Jarvis that proposes and coordinates work through controlled tools. It does not spend money, file documents, post accounting entries, reject workers, or hard-delete records autonomously.

## Recommended launch offer

The figures below remain pilot hypotheses, not executable software defaults. V0 stores versioned,
human-accepted fee rules. It must not preselect a percentage, minimum, reserve, tax rate, or markup
until the owner contract contains that exact rule. This prevents the development harness from
turning an unresolved commercial experiment into billing behavior.

### Operations tier

- 12% of accommodation revenue, with a minimum monthly fee of ₹3,500 per active property.
- Calendar ingestion, task scheduling, owner dashboard, inventory visibility, and incident coordination.
- The homeowner retains listing strategy, guest messaging, and revenue management.
- Cleaning, laundry, consumables, repairs, and purchased goods are separate.

### Full-service tier

- 18% of accommodation revenue, with a minimum monthly fee of ₹6,000 per active property.
- Includes Operations tier features plus guest-operations coordination, quality assurance, vendor management, and monthly owner reporting.
- Cleaning, laundry, consumables, repairs, and purchased goods remain separate.

The percentage base should be defined as accommodation revenue excluding taxes, refundable deposits, and pass-through cleaning charges. This avoids hidden commission on reimbursed costs. External industry references commonly place hands-on short-term-rental management above a software-only service, often around 15%-25%, but the UP launch price must be validated against the 30-property cohort [S15].

### Turnover pricing hypothesis

Use a size-based rate card and validate it in the pilot:

| Property | Proposed starting price | Exclusions |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Studio or 1 bedroom | ₹799 | Laundry, replacement goods, exceptional damage |
| 2 bedrooms | ₹999 | Laundry, replacement goods, exceptional damage |
| 3 bedrooms | ₹1,299 | Laundry, replacement goods, exceptional damage |
| Same-day rush | +25% | Subject to crew capacity |

These are pilot hypotheses, not established local market facts. No public launch should occur until actual task times, route time, wage obligations, and owner willingness to pay have been measured.

## Planning economics for 30 properties

This model is intentionally simple. It is for deciding whether the concept deserves a pilot, not for investor reporting.

Assumptions:

- 30 properties on Full-service.
- Average management fee: 18% of accommodation revenue.
- Average turnover invoice: ₹950.
- Two core field employees at launch, with independent service businesses covering peaks.
- Each core field employee costs approximately ₹21,000 per month including employer-side burden and benefits in the planning model.
- Fixed operating cost includes field payroll, an operations lead, modest founder draw, software, accounting, insurance, bikes, a micro-hub, marketing, and contingency.
- GST, income tax, financing cost, and extraordinary claims are excluded.

| Monthly scenario | Conservative | Base | Strong |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Accommodation revenue per property | ₹35,000 | ₹55,000 | ₹80,000 |
| Management revenue at 18% | ₹189,000 | ₹297,000 | ₹432,000 |
| Turnovers per property | 2.5 | 3.5 | 4.5 |
| Turnover billings | ₹71,250 | ₹99,750 | ₹128,250 |
| Supply gross margin | ₹7,200 | ₹10,800 | ₹16,200 |
| Maintenance coordination fees | ₹3,750 | ₹5,000 | ₹7,500 |
| Total before direct goods and taxes | ₹271,200 | ₹412,550 | ₹583,950 |
| Planning operating cost | ₹205,000 | ₹221,000 | ₹248,000 |
| Indicative operating surplus | ₹66,200 | ₹191,550 | ₹335,950 |

The apparent surplus is highly sensitive to real booking revenue, property clustering, laundry cost, owner churn, and the number of urgent visits. The gate is not whether the spreadsheet looks profitable. The gate is whether a six-week pilot produces positive contribution per property after recording every labor minute and kilometer.

## Stage gates

### Gate 0: legal and entity readiness

Required before worker onboarding or owner billing:

- UP lawyer confirms entity, labor model, worker agreements, vehicle custody, property access, liability, and the applicable Shops and Establishments and labor-code registrations.
- Chartered Accountant confirms GST treatment, invoice structure, payroll, ESI/EPF triggers, inventory accounting, and whether any e-commerce-operator obligations apply.
- Each property has a compliance checklist for the UP Bed & Breakfast and Homestay Policy 2025 or other applicable accommodation regime [S5].
- Insurance broker confirms employee accident, public liability, property-key/access, stock, cyber, and e-bike coverage.

### Gate 1: commercial proof

- At least 15 signed pilot letters from the known 30-property cohort.
- Ninety days of anonymized booking, turnover, incident, supply, and owner-cost data.
- Owners accept the scope boundary and separate pass-through charges.
- Median modeled contribution is at least ₹3,000 per property per month.

### Gate 2: five-property pilot

Run for six weeks with two clusters, no minors, no paid ads, no external marketplace, and no imported vehicles.

Pass criteria:

- At least 95% of scheduled turnovers started within the service window.
- At least 92% first-pass quality acceptance.
- Fewer than 2% critical stockout events.
- Zero unlogged property-access events.
- Zero unapproved purchases.
- All worker time and travel are recorded and paid lawfully.
- Positive property contribution after all direct cost.
- Owner satisfaction at least 8/10, with no unresolved high-severity complaint older than 48 hours.

### Gate 3: 15-property controlled launch

- At least eight consecutive weeks meeting the pilot service levels.
- Two months of cash runway after committed hiring and inventory.
- Jarvis audit log demonstrates that every AI action was scoped and reversible.
- Repeatable route clusters and peak-capacity plan.

### Gate 4: 30-property operating launch

- Add micro-inventory, a second e-bike, and structured supplier agreements.
- Consider sponsored catalog placements only after owner trust and procurement quality are stable.
- Do not add the public worker marketplace or external accounting service at this gate.
