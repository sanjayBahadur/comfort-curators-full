# Stage 0 Feasibility Analysis

## 1. Business thesis

Comfort Curators can be viable if it behaves as an operations company with a focused software layer. It is unlikely to be viable at launch as a simultaneous property manager, labor marketplace, vehicle financier, importer, retail marketplace, advertising network, warehouse operator, and accounting-services company.

The narrow thesis is:

> Independent short-term-rental owners will pay Comfort Curators to make property operations predictable, evidenced, and low-touch. Comfort Curators earns recurring management revenue, task revenue, and supply margin while using software to lower coordination cost.

The moat is not the LLM. The moat is the local operating network, property-specific knowledge, controlled access, response reliability, purchasing data, quality evidence, and owner trust.

## 2. Customer and positioning

### Primary customer

- Owner or co-host of one to ten short-term-rental units.
- Already receives bookings but struggles with turnover reliability, restocking, maintenance, evidence, and remote oversight.
- Values brand quality and wants a repeatable guest experience.
- Will pay for reduced operational failure, not for an abstract AI dashboard.

### Positioning

Comfort Curators should be presented as:

> The documented local operations layer for independent short-term-rental hosts in Uttar Pradesh.

Avoid presenting it as a gig app or cheap labor source. That weakens owner trust, worker quality, and the premium brand.

### Commercial promise

- A prepared property before every arrival.
- A visible audit trail of what was checked, used, replaced, and approved.
- One accountable route for incidents.
- A customizable property package with predictable replenishment.

## 3. Revenue model

### Keep as core revenue

1. Percentage management fee with a minimum monthly fee.
2. Turnover and inspection fees.
3. Maintenance coordination fee or documented markup.
4. Gross margin on consumables and approved goods.
5. One-time property onboarding and setup fee.

### Defer as optional revenue

1. Sponsored catalog placement and vendor rebates.
2. External homeowner marketplace for independent specialists.
3. Optional e-bike ownership program for workers.
4. Bookkeeping support sold to third parties.

### Reject as launch revenue

1. Worker registration fees.
2. Mandatory equipment sales to workers.
3. Hidden supplier commissions.
4. Statutory audit services without a properly appointed Chartered Accountant.
5. Passenger transport by delivery or maintenance workers.

## 4. Workforce model

### Core crew

Cleaning, inspections, restocking, access handling, and routine property tasks should be performed by fixed-term employees when Comfort Curators controls:

- when and where the person works;
- the service checklist and quality standard;
- the price charged to the owner;
- the uniform, equipment, or e-bike;
- access credentials or keys;
- performance review and corrective action; and
- assignment priority.

Calling this person a contractor does not remove the operational reality. The labor codes are effective nationally from 21 November 2025, require appointment letters, set wage and overtime protections, and create threshold-based obligations for ESI, EPF, gratuity, and other protections [S1][S2].

Recommended launch staffing:

- Two fixed-term Curators for five to fifteen clustered properties.
- One operations lead.
- A panel of independent specialist businesses and an overflow cleaning business.
- Add the third core Curator only after measured workload supports it.

### Genuine independent vendors

Use independent vendors for work where the vendor:

- serves multiple customers;
- sets or negotiates its own price;
- supplies its own skilled tools and insurance;
- can accept or decline work;
- controls how the result is produced, subject to safety and acceptance criteria; and
- invoices Comfort Curators or the owner as a business.

Examples include licensed electrical work, major plumbing, appliance repair, pest control, structural repair, locksmith work, and specialized deep cleaning.

### Young workers

The Child and Adolescent Labour law prohibits employment of children below 14 and prohibits adolescents aged 14-18 in hazardous occupations and processes [S3]. That does not make client-home entry, road delivery, late shifts, cash handling, and lone work a good 16-17-year-old employment program.

The DPDP Act also treats anyone under 18 as a child, with parental-consent and child-data restrictions scheduled to become broadly relevant as the phased provisions commence [S6].

Decision: require all operational workers to be 18 or older in the MVP.

A later youth program may be considered only as a separate, supervised, daylight training or apprenticeship track with no solo property access, no night delivery, no hazardous chemicals, and no performance tracking that violates child-data rules.

### Worker onboarding principles

- No application fee or registration deposit.
- Plain-language Hindi and English terms.
- Appointment letter or vendor agreement before work.
- Role classification based on actual duties.
- Minimum wage and overtime checks against the current UP notification and local zone.
- Clear wage period, task incentive, expense, deduction, and grievance rules.
- Background and reference checks proportionate to home access.
- Safety training, chemical handling, access control, and emergency response.
- Human review before rejection, suspension, or deactivation.
- No rating-only termination.

The Code on Wages limits deductions and requires authorized categories and process. A vehicle-loss or damage process cannot become an unrestricted wage-recovery mechanism [S1][S2].

## 5. E-bike model

### Legal premise

ARAI states that electric bikes below the applicable 250 W and 25 km/h limits are exempt from the approval and licensing treatment that applies to higher-powered or faster electric two-wheelers [S4]. The actual vehicle, controller, firmware, and maximum design speed must comply. A marketing sticker saying “250 W” is not enough.

### Procurement decision

Start with two locally sold, supported, compliant models. Current listed Indian examples show roughly ₹29,000-₹37,000 retail for 250 W products before cargo modifications [S14]. Seek a fleet quote, but prioritize:

- Indian warranty service within the operating district;
- removable, traceable battery packs;
- battery and charger safety documentation;
- mechanical parts available for at least three years;
- a declared payload compatible with worker plus cargo;
- weather protection and braking appropriate to loaded use;
- speed limitation that cannot be casually disabled;
- telematics that can be disabled outside work hours; and
- fire-safe charging and battery storage instructions.

### Why direct China import is deferred

Direct import may appear cheaper at factory price but adds customs classification, applicable component and battery compliance, freight, damaged-unit risk, spares, warranty administration, charger compatibility, quality control, and product-liability exposure. A small first fleet has too little purchasing power to absorb these costs.

Consider import only when:

- the fleet requirement is at least 20 bikes;
- an Indian compliance professional verifies the exact model and battery requirements;
- landed three-year cost is at least 20% below a comparable supported Indian fleet quote;
- replacement batteries and safety-critical parts are stocked; and
- an Indian service and recall process exists.

### Worker economics

A ₹30,000 bike cannot be responsibly repaid from a worker’s first month if the worker’s total monthly compensation is in the same general range as UP entry-level wages. Forcing such repayment creates debt dependence and likely increases attrition.

For core employees:

- company owns the bike;
- bike is free work equipment;
- worker signs a custody and safety agreement;
- ordinary wear is a company cost;
- loss or deliberate damage follows a documented investigation and lawful recovery rules;
- company pays maintenance, safety gear, and work charging.

For a later voluntary ownership plan:

- no tie to continued employment;
- no balloon payment;
- transparent cash price and total repayment;
- 18-24 month term;
- easy return and fair residual-value calculation;
- deductions far below the statutory maximum and reviewed by payroll counsel; and
- no loss of earned wages or deposits on exit.

### Safety and use limits

- No passenger transport.
- No high-value cash or restricted-goods delivery.
- No alcohol, tobacco, controlled medicines, or identity-document errands in the MVP.
- Daylight routes preferred until incident data justifies later coverage.
- Helmet, lights, reflective equipment, first aid, phone mount, cargo restraint, and emergency contact required.
- On-duty location only; no continuous off-duty tracking.

## 6. Property and hospitality compliance

The UP Bed & Breakfast and Homestay Policy 2025 formalizes registered homestays and describes a one-to-six-room, up-to-twelve-bed model in covered circumstances [S5]. Comfort Curators should not assume every short-term listing qualifies or that the platform listing itself proves legality.

Every property must have a compliance record containing:

- ownership or management authority;
- applicable homestay, B&B, hotel, paying-guest, society, municipal, fire, police, or other permission;
- approved room and bed count;
- tax and invoicing status;
- guest identification and record obligations;
- emergency contacts and safety equipment;
- prohibited use and occupancy rules;
- insurance confirmation; and
- expiration and renewal dates.

Comfort Curators should reserve the contractual right to refuse service to a property that lacks required permission or creates unsafe work conditions.

## 7. Package and micro-inventory model

### Homeowner experience

The homeowner should build a package like a controlled cart:

1. Select property type and occupancy.
2. Choose a baseline service level.
3. Select room-level experience kits.
4. Set preferred brands, house-standard products, and substitutions.
5. Set per-order and monthly budgets.
6. Review predicted monthly burn rate.
7. Approve one-time setup and recurring replenishment.

Suggested bundles:

- Essential Turnover.
- Guest Comfort.
- Premium Bath.
- Long Stay.
- Family Stay.
- Local Welcome.

Each product must show unit price, pack size, source, expected usage, substitution rule, and whether it is a house-standard or sponsored item.

### Inventory strategy

Do not lease a conventional warehouse for 30 properties. Use a secure micro-fulfillment point with 20-40 high-turn SKUs.

Stock first:

- cleaning consumables;
- toilet tissue and paper products;
- sealed toiletries;
- trash liners;
- basic maintenance consumables;
- standardized guest items; and
- selected linen reserve if laundry operations justify it.

Use local distributors and vendor-managed supply where possible. Private-label products should wait until product consumption is stable.

If food is stored, sold, distributed, or offered through e-commerce, determine the applicable FSSAI registration or licence for each business activity and premise. The April 2026 FoSCoS eligibility schedule separately recognizes storage, distribution, retail, transport, and e-commerce activities [S8]. The simplest MVP boundary is sealed shelf-stable goods only, with no prepared food.

### Replenishment logic

The supply system should calculate:

`reorder point = expected lead-time consumption + safety stock`

The LLM may explain anomalies and draft a purchase proposal. It may not be the source of truth for stock, approve its own order, or place an unbounded purchase.

## 8. Partner ads and supplier rebates

Sponsored catalog placement can create margin but also creates a conflict with the Curators brand.

Rules:

- Every sponsored item is labeled “Sponsored”.
- Operational search ranking and quality recommendations remain independent.
- Owners can hide sponsored products.
- Rebate and affiliate income is recorded per supplier and SKU.
- No guest or worker personal data is shared for advertising.
- Claims must be substantiated.
- Environmental claims such as “zero-carbon stay” require a documented basis. CCPA has specific greenwashing guidance [S11].

Do not include ad revenue in the first-year base forecast.

## 9. Documenthand and accounting

### Documenthand

Keep this capability, but define it as a controlled document workflow:

- collect required documents;
- extract metadata with confidence labels;
- check expiry and completeness;
- generate a review packet;
- route to owner, operations, CA, lawyer, or insurer;
- record approval, rejection, and comments; and
- export or submit only when a human with authority confirms.

No LLM should sign, certify, or silently file a regulated document.

### Accounting crew

Young accounting professionals can perform bookkeeping, reconciliation, invoice preparation, document checking, management reporting, and audit-preparation work under supervision. They should not be marketed as statutory auditors. Under Companies Act requirements, statutory company audit and signature require an eligible Chartered Accountant or CA firm [S12].

Safe AI uses:

- suggest invoice coding;
- identify duplicate invoices;
- reconcile inventory movements to approved tickets;
- draft exception summaries;
- flag missing support; and
- prepare month-end checklists.

Prohibited AI uses:

- approve or release payment;
- create a vendor and pay it in one flow;
- post final journals without review;
- change bank details from an email request;
- certify tax or audit conclusions;
- conceal low-confidence extraction; or
- send owner financial data to an unapproved consumer AI account.

Company workers should receive company-controlled devices or an optional BYOD arrangement. Selling laptops or subscriptions as a condition of work adds a debt and licensing business that does not improve the launch value proposition.

## 10. Tax and marketplace boundary

Comfort Curators should receive a written CA memo that separates:

- property-management service revenue;
- cleaning and maintenance services;
- pass-through owner reimbursements;
- sale of goods;
- vendor commissions or rebates;
- e-commerce-operator collection, if any;
- accommodation taxes borne by the owner or platform; and
- worker or vendor payments.

CBIC guidance recognizes a general ₹20 lakh registration threshold for many service suppliers, while e-commerce, notified services, and operator-specific rules can change the result. CBIC also lists 18% for many maintenance and repair services and special treatment for some housekeeping supplied through an e-commerce operator [S9]. Do not hard-code one GST rate across every line item.

The owner invoice must identify taxable supplier, service period, property, line item, tax, reimbursement, and payment status. Never hide supplier rebates or convert employee wages into “vendor reimbursements.”

## 11. Data, identity, and AI compliance

### Data collected

The platform will hold sensitive operational data even if it does not process legally defined special categories:

- worker identity and bank details;
- owner identity and contracts;
- property address, access methods, and occupancy schedule;
- guest incidents and communications;
- location and task evidence;
- invoices and financial records; and
- background-check results.

### DPDP readiness

The DPDP Act has phased commencement. Most substantive sections listed in the November 2025 notification are scheduled for eighteen months after publication, while other sections came into effect earlier [S6]. The system should be built to the final operating standard now rather than requiring a privacy rewrite in 2027.

Required design:

- purpose-specific notice;
- minimal collection;
- withdrawal and erasure workflow subject to legal retention;
- processor contracts;
- access, correction, and grievance process;
- encryption and access controls;
- breach response; and
- data inventory and retention schedule.

Aadhaar must be voluntary where used for private identity verification, with a viable alternate method. Offline Aadhaar verification requires consent, digital-signature verification, masking, secure storage, and deletion on withdrawal where permitted [S7]. Store a verification result and minimal masked reference, not an unrestricted Aadhaar image library.

### AI limits

- AI output is advisory unless a coded policy explicitly permits the action.
- No direct database credentials for the model.
- Tools enforce tenant, property, role, budget, and state transitions.
- Low-confidence extraction triggers human review.
- Worker eligibility and discipline require human decisions.
- Owner-facing recommendations disclose sponsorship and uncertainty.
- Model prompts and output never become the sole audit record.

## 12. Risk register

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Decision or mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Worker misclassification | High | High | Fixed-term employment for controlled core work; vendor agreements for independent businesses |
| Underpayment or unlawful deductions | Medium | High | Current wage table, time records, overtime, wage slips, deduction review |
| Minor safety and data exposure | High if allowed | High | 18+ operational workforce in MVP |
| Home access theft or allegation | Medium | High | Verified identity, scoped access, event logs, two-person rule for high-risk entry, insurance |
| E-bike battery or road incident | Medium | High | Supported model, safety documentation, training, charging controls, accident cover |
| Imported fleet failure | High at small scale | Medium | Local procurement first; import gate at 20+ bikes and 20% lifecycle advantage |
| Property lacks valid permission | Medium | High | Compliance record and right to refuse service |
| AI creates or deletes harmful work | Medium | High | Draft-first tools, approval thresholds, no hard delete, immutable audit events |
| Inventory shrinkage or expiry | Medium | Medium | Lot/location ledger, counts, sealed stock, reconciliation |
| Sponsored ranking damages trust | Medium | High | Explicit label, separate operational ranking, opt-out |
| Accounting or tax error | Medium | High | Licensed system of record, maker-checker approval, CA review |
| Data breach or key exposure | Medium | High | Encryption, least privilege, masked identity, incident process, CERT-In plan [S13] |
| Route density too low | High | High | Cluster-only pilot and travel-time costing |
| Owner acquisition without data | Medium | High | LOIs and ninety-day data before fixed spend |
| Scope expansion destroys execution | High | High | Phase gates and written out-of-scope list |

## 13. Go or no-go recommendation

Conditional go.

Proceed to a five-property, six-week pilot only if Gate 0 and Gate 1 are met. Stop or redesign if any of the following is true:

- fewer than 15 owners sign pilot letters;
- properties are geographically scattered enough that travel consumes more than 25% of paid field time;
- owners reject separate cleaning and consumable charges;
- median property contribution is below ₹3,000 per month;
- legal review does not support the proposed worker and property-access model;
- supported e-bike and insurance coverage cannot be obtained; or
- the pilot cannot maintain a complete access and approval audit trail.
